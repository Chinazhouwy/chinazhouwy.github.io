# The Guardian view on the National Year of Reading 2026: time to start a healthy habit for life

This page is an independently written reading guide to The Guardian's editorial, first published online on 29 December 2025. The paper labels the piece “Editorial”; no individual author is named. Follow the original-article link below for the publisher's full text.

The editorial welcomes the UK government's plan to make 2026 the National Year of Reading. It argues that reading with children can support learning and relationships, while pointing to a decline in reading for pleasure among young people. The article presents this not simply as a school issue, but as a habit shaped by families and public institutions.

It highlights several ways to make books more accessible to children, including distributing books, introducing a children's literary prize and providing library access from an early age. These proposals differ in scale, but each aims to make reading a more familiar part of childhood rather than an activity reserved for already enthusiastic readers.

The editorial also stresses that encouragement alone cannot remove the barriers families face. Poverty, reduced access to local libraries, long working hours and the cost or availability of books can all make shared reading difficult. Its argument therefore moves from celebrating a national campaign to asking whether public services and practical support will make that campaign work.

Here is an editor-written practice sentence, not a quotation from the original article: A reading campaign can reach more children when families have both appealing books and accessible places to borrow them.

As you read, separate the editorial's goals from the means it proposes. Notice how it moves from a public campaign to examples of programs, then to the resources needed to sustain them; this helps distinguish a policy announcement from the conditions required for lasting change.
