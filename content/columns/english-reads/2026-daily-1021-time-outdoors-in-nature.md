# Half of UK adults say they spend less than three hours a week outside in nature

This page is an independently written reading guide to Damian Carrington's article for The Guardian, first published online on 25 May 2026. Follow the original-article link below for the publisher's full text.

The article opens with a contrast between adults' positive memories of outdoor play and the limited time many now spend in natural settings. As you read, distinguish the poll's reported figures from the article's wider discussion of health and access to green space.

Look at how the piece moves from individual habits to public conditions: parks can offer nearby contact with nature, but access is unequal and local budgets affect their upkeep. Notice which points come from the survey, which are attributed to named sources, and which describe government commitments.

Here is an editor-written practice sentence, not a quotation from the original article: Although nearby parks can make outdoor time easier to fit into a busy week, their value depends on whether residents can reach them and whether local services have the resources to maintain them.

After reading, consider what the survey can—and cannot—tell us about why people spend less time outdoors. Then ask how childhood memories, access to public green space, and the funding of parks fit into the article's argument.
