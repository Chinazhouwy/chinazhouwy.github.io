# AI Eases Our Mental Load at the Expense of Critical Thinking

This page is a reading guide to an article by The Open Minds Foundation, published in Psychology Today on 30 October 2024. Follow the original-article link below to read the publisher's full text.

As you read, keep two questions separate: how much effort does finding an answer require, and how carefully does the reader examine it? An easier task and a deeper understanding are different goals. Look for the comparison made in the student study, then identify the limits that the article acknowledges.

Here is an editor-written practice sentence, rather than a quotation from the original article: Although a tool can make information easier to obtain, a learner who accepts its answer without checking other sources may miss the reasoning that makes the answer useful.

Consider three questions after reading the original: What does the study directly compare? Which limitations affect how widely its findings can be applied? Where does the writer move from reporting research to making a broader argument? Keep your answers tied to evidence rather than assuming that every claim has the same level of support.
