# The Hater's Guide to Loving Boredom

This page is an independently written reading guide to Heather Lench's article for Psychology Today, posted on 18 June 2025. Follow the original-article link below for the publisher's full text.

Lench distinguishes boredom from ordinary rest: it can arise when nothing is happening, or even when activity is all around us but fails to engage us. The piece asks readers to treat boredom not simply as something to escape, but as an emotion that can be noticed and understood.

The guide to responding has three steps: recognize the feeling, appreciate the motivation it creates, and choose what to do with that motivation. The author notes that the urge for novelty can lead to unhelpful habits, but it may also be directed toward more constructive activities. The emphasis is on making an intentional choice rather than reacting automatically.

Here is an editor-written practice sentence, not a quotation from the original article: Once people recognize the restlessness that boredom creates, they may be better able to direct that energy toward activities that serve their longer-term goals.

As you read, notice how the article moves from defining boredom to suggesting a response. Consider whether naming an uncomfortable feeling can change the choices that follow, and distinguish the author's practical advice from any broader claim about emotional well-being.
