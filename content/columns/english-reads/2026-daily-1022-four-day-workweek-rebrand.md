# Bosses don’t like the sound of a ‘four-day workweek’. Maybe it’s time to rebrand it

This page is an independently written reading guide to Gene Marks's article for The Guardian, first published online on 26 April 2026. Follow the original-article link below for the publisher's full text.

The article considers why some employers resist the idea of reducing the working week while keeping pay unchanged. As you read, separate objections to the label from questions about workload, output, and what employers regard as a fair exchange.

Marks also suggests that flexible schedules and paid time off may already give some workers a shorter week in practice, even when employers do not describe them that way. Notice how the piece moves from workplace attitudes to examples of existing arrangements, and distinguish the writer's opinion from claims about broader trends.

Here is an editor-written practice sentence, not a quotation from the original article: Although a shorter schedule may sound costly to an employer, flexible arrangements can be easier to accept when they are linked to completed work and employee benefits.

After reading, consider whether changing the name of a policy can change how people judge it. Then weigh the article's examples against its argument about productivity, fairness, and the language used to describe working time.
